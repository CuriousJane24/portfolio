Customer experience case study · Group 5

Ijeoma Home & Living

Customer Journey, Experience & Satisfaction

Making the buying journey more predictable through clear communication, reliable handoffs and coordinated follow-up.

My role: Quality Assurance and Automation team member.

The challenge

The case centred on a made-to-order furniture business whose customers valued the products but experienced uncertainty throughout the buying journey.

Customers encountered slow responses, inconsistent pricing information, missing written confirmations and limited updates during production and around installation.

The team identified a central issue: important agreements and instructions were not consistently documented, confirmed and passed between teams.

Understanding the customer journey

We mapped six stages of the customer journey:

  1. Awareness
  2. Consideration
  3. Purchase
  4. Production
  5. Delivery
  6. Post-purchase

Price, payment and delivery emerged as key moments when customers needed clear, consistent information to feel confident.

The team's approach

Confirm the order

Capture order details, provide written confirmation and obtain customer approval before releasing the order to production.

Keep the delivery promise clear

Maintain one committed delivery date and connect production updates, quality checks, delivery and installation instructions.

Follow feedback through to resolution

Capture customer feedback, assign responsibility, take action, close the issue and use the learning to improve the experience.

The approach used Zoho for CRM and automation, alongside a retention strategy and win-back automation.

  • Followed up on the slide deck and outstanding contributions.
  • Held meetings, listened to suggestions and followed up with inactive teammates to support participation and completion.

  • Created an illustrative AI video to support the team's presentation.

Deliverables and proposed rollout

The team's work included a customer journey map, a customer experience strategy, a retention approach and a presentation bringing the recommendations together.

The proposed rollout covered three phases:

  • Days 0–30: strengthen order capture and confirmation.
  • Days 31–60: improve delivery commitments and communication.
  • Days 61–90: strengthen feedback resolution and continuous improvement.

Improved confirmation, delivery clarity, fewer chase calls and fewer installation incidents were proposed targets. This case study does not claim measured improvements in customer satisfaction or retention.
