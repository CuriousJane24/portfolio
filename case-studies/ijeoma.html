Customer Experience Case Study · Group 5

Ijeoma Home & Living: Customer Journey, Experience & Satisfaction

Customers valued Ijeoma’s furniture, but buying a custom piece left them with questions. The price quoted online could differ from the showroom price. Even after paying a deposit, a customer could still be unsure what had been agreed or when to expect delivery.

My role: Quality assurance and automation team member
Key skills: Customer journey mapping, quality assurance and team coordination
CRM: Zoho CRM

1. Where customers lost confidence

Ijeoma Home & Living is a made-to-order furniture business. The case describes three showrooms, a growing online presence and more than 9,200 customers. Our team reviewed where the buying experience broke down across these channels.

  • Enquiry and pricing. Customers waited for replies online and received different prices in the showroom without a clear explanation.
  • Payment and confirmation. One customer paid a 60% deposit but had no written confirmation of the fabric, dimensions or delivery date.
  • Production and installation. Customers had to chase updates, and special instructions did not always reach the installers.
  • Post-purchase follow-up. Customers needed a clear point of contact when something went wrong after delivery.

We found that an order could move into production before anyone had recorded the agreement and checked it with the customer. That made written confirmation a priority.

2. My contribution

I worked on the Quality Assurance and Automation teams. My responsibility was to check that each team delivered what the brief called for and that our recommendations fitted together across the customer journey.

  • Quality assurance. I reviewed each team’s contribution and followed up on gaps as we brought the slide deck together.
  • Team coordination. I held meetings,gathered suggestions and followed up with teammates who had outstanding work.
  • Presentation. I created an illustrative AI video to support the team’s presentation.
  • Zoho CRM. I reviewed the Zoho win-back automation and coordinated its completion.

3. How we mapped the journey

We mapped six stages: awareness, consideration, purchase,production, delivery and post-purchase follow-up. Three moments mattered most for customer confidence. At pricing, the customerneeded a consistent quote. At payment, they needed a record of their order. At delivery, they needed a date they could plan around.

We recommended one shared order record in Zoho CRM. The team would record the agreed price, fabric, dimensions and delivery expectations, then ask the customer to confirm the details before production began.

During production, customers would receive progress updates and hear about changes to the delivery date. Installation instructions would travel with the order. After delivery, each issue would have an owner responsible for following it through to resolution.

4. What we found and how we would measure progress

The case data showed how much information was lost between payment and delivery. Of 310 orders with a deposit, 248 reached a completed consultation, 97 reached written confirmation and only 43 reached the point where the delivery date was clear.Those 43 orders represented 13.9% of the original 310.

We delivered a customer journey map, a retention strategy and a proposed 90-day rollout. The plan had three phases.

Days 0–30. Record the order and obtain written customer confirmation before production.
Days 31–60. Agree on a delivery date and keep the customer informed as the order progresses.
Days 61–90. Assign feedback to an owner,follow each issue to resolution and review recurring problems.

To assess the rollout, we proposed tracking how many orders received written confirmation and a clear delivery date, how often customers had to call for updates, and how many installation incidents occurred. These were targets for the proposed rollout.
